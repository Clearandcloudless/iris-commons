## Turn every AI assistant into a doorway to public collaboration

Every day, people hold extraordinary conversations with AI. New theories are proposed. Institutional failures are uncovered. Public-interest projects are designed. Difficult ideas are explained in languages that global platforms rarely hear.

Most of those ideas disappear inside private chat windows, temporary context, platform accounts and language barriers.

**Iris Commons exists to change that.**

We are exploring a human–AI public-collaboration paradigm that depends only on GitHub, open document protocols and the AI chosen by each participant. Anyone should be able to think in their own language, work with the AI they already trust—GPT, Gemini, Claude, a local model or a future agent—and participate in a public project without first mastering Git, GitHub, academic jargon or a global lingua franca.

The AI becomes the participant's secretary, translator, researcher and critic. GitHub becomes the public, auditable record of how ideas are proposed, challenged, revised, tested and accepted.

You should not need to learn the machinery before contributing to the thinking.

**You only need to know how to talk to an AI.**

```text
Your language and your real ideas
                ↓
       Your personal AI secretary
                ↓
 Translate · Organize · Research · Challenge · Coordinate
                ↓
 Human discussion, review and decisions on GitHub
                ↓
 Traceable, reviewable and continuously evolving public knowledge
```

## A reusable template, not a central authority

Iris Commons does not require all research to enter one global knowledge base, and it does not govern projects created from this template.

Any team may copy or adapt this repository to create an independent project around its own public topic. Each copied project has its own name, scope, maintainers, governance, knowledge base and release cycle:

- it does not need to register with Iris Commons;
- it does not need to connect or synchronize with other copied projects;
- it does not need to submit its research results back to Iris Commons;
- it may modify the template, while making its current rules clear to its own participants;
- it may voluntarily contribute generally useful improvements upstream.

Iris Commons maintains a reusable collaboration method: people express intent, AI lowers participation barriers, and GitHub preserves public reasoning, review and version history.

## What if ideas no longer needed passports?

In the collaboration model Iris Commons is exploring:

- a Chinese-speaking engineer could use AI to contribute to a Spanish-language public-safety research project;
- a teacher unfamiliar with GitHub could ask an AI to find the right discussion and prepare a carefully reviewed contribution;
- a member of the public could ask for a clear explanation of a specialist dispute instead of accepting an opaque summary;
- different models could translate, investigate, criticize and verify without pretending that model agreement is evidence;
- every important claim could preserve its original wording, context, sources, objections and revision history;
- minority views would not disappear merely because they were written in a less dominant language or less professional style.

This is not merely a knowledge base and not merely a multilingual forum.

It could become a new kind of public collaboration infrastructure:

> **A way for independent teams to use the AIs they choose, think together without erasing linguistic, cultural or intellectual differences, and preserve the full process on GitHub.**

## Why Iris?

Iris is the rainbow messenger of Greek mythology.

A rainbow connects separated worlds without blending every color into one.

That is the central metaphor of Iris Commons:

- connection without assimilation;
- translation without erasure;
- collaboration without obedience;
- shared knowledge that still remembers where each idea came from and what it survived.

## What we are building

Iris Commons is gradually developing:

- a reusable GitHub repository structure and document protocol;
- an AI-first participation path for people who do not use GitHub;
- a communication protocol connecting Discussions, Issues, comments, Pull Requests, reviews and long-lived knowledge;
- mechanisms for triage, goal initiation, prioritization, independent review, formal releases and appeals;
- a multilingual model linking original text, translations, summaries and terminology;
- research records that humans and AI assistants can both understand;
- auditable permissions and disclosure rules for AI secretaries;
- open formats portable across model providers, Git platforms, research services and local infrastructure;
- practical methods tested through real public projects.

Core protocols:

- [`AI_PARTICIPATION.md`](AI_PARTICIPATION.md): how participants enter a project through their own AI;
- [`COMMUNICATION_PROTOCOL.md`](COMMUNICATION_PROTOCOL.md): how GitHub objects divide responsibilities and connect;
- [`TEAM_GOVERNANCE.md`](TEAM_GOVERNANCE.md): team roles, prioritization, project initiation, review and release;
- [`CONTENT_LIFECYCLE.md`](CONTENT_LIFECYCLE.md): how public discussion becomes long-lived knowledge;
- [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md): minimum governance, permission and responsibility rules.

## One boundary we will not compromise

Iris Commons pursues ideas without borders—not data without boundaries.

Privacy, copyright, research ethics, real-world safety and human authorization always take precedence over automation and AI efficiency.

AI may become a bridge, secretary, translator and critic. It must never become an unauthorized spokesperson or the final judge of truth.
